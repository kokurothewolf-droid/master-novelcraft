# Character psychology, agency and conflicts

**Sources:** Moore-attributed character transcript; `sw-character-conflict` §§1-7, `sw-japanese-screenwriting` §§4-5, `fiction-writing-story-development(1).md`, `character-management(1).md`, Chinese-language `SKILL(9).md` character-distillation model.

## Character as a lived self, not a list of traits

Build the character at five layers: **speech**, **world model**, **decision heuristics**, **moral boundaries and exceptions**, and **blind spots/self-deception**. Gather evidence from direct dialogue, choices, inner thought (when available), others' opinions (which may be wrong), relationship differences, and pivotal decisions. Test a profile against at least two separate situations before calling a trait stable. A character profile can include appearance and life history, but the reader usually experiences traits through decisions and perception.

## A useable character profile

Record: name/aliases/pronunciation, age and appearance, physical embodiment (abilities, fatigue, sensory emphasis), social position/history, conscious desires and fears, hidden longings, worldview, contradiction, relationship-specific behaviors, speech (diction, rhythm, verbal avoidance), strengths, boundaries, current misconception, potential conflicts, secrets/knowledge, and temporal changes. Use the template, but do not fill unknown fields with invented canon.

### Desire without forced quest

A character may want something large (justice, power, peace, rescue) or small (a bath alone, respect at dinner, to conceal embarrassment). They may not know what they want. A steadfast or slice-of-life protagonist may react more than pursue; story interest can arise from relationship variation, self-deception or a different person's change. **D3 remains author's choice**, not a contradiction smoothed by an arbitrary blanket rule.

## Character agency under pressure

Give significant supporting people their own values and capacity for choice. Avoid villains whose only purpose is evil. Meaningful pressure can be sorrow, temptation, disagreement, affection, absurdity, scarcity, mistaken assumptions, social obligations or material systems. It should reveal something through what the person does, not a lecture about who they are. No fixed cruelty quota.

### Conflict map

For each person in a difficult exchange: immediate want; perceived obstacle; what they think the other wants; what they refuse to admit; strategy; threshold for retreat; relationship history; what each stands to lose. Oppositions may be direct (both seek the same position) or orthogonal (one wants solitude, another company). For comedy, a character can pursue a tiny objective with enormous personal seriousness.

## Temporal integrity

Every canonical statement needs its time scope. A relationship changes from friends to estranged in chapter 8, or a character discovers a secret in chapter 12, without rewriting earlier ignorance. Distinguish:

- Objective canon and physical facts.
- A character's private belief, false belief or deliberate lie.
- What the reader currently knows.
- Author's provisional plan versus approved event.

When checking an earlier chapter, apply the **character knowledge and physical state as of that chapter**, not later revelations.

## Direct-speech guardrails

Give distinct voices through perceptions, priorities, idioms, syntax and attitudes toward the listener. Not everyone needs a catchphrase or tic. Pronunciation and cultural register must be consistent with the author's design. Let emotionally intense characters sometimes speak plainly. Subtext is useful, but perpetual evasiveness can feel artificial.

## Character extraction from an existing novel

Read sufficient actual pages. Collect evidence by scene and chapter, build the five layers, identify apparent contradictions, and test against described decisions. Do not infer unprovided trauma or motives as canon. Label inferences with confidence and supporting scene; ask the author before installing major guesses in the bible.

## Revision review

Flag reactions that are inconsistent *without a visible cause*, identical voices, unearned moral flips, major decisions made to satisfy plot convenience, and supporting characters without human self-interest. Suggest alternative actions consistent with present motives. Never change accepted character facts silently.
