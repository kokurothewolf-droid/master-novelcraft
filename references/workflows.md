# Task execution recipes

Use these when the task is complex; do not run every step for simple requests.

## W1. Start a novel

1. Recover author-provided genre, character concerns, reader promise, setting, themes, POV/tense and boundaries. Ask only for critical absences.
2. Generate two or three differentiated directions *if options are requested*; otherwise advance the chosen concept.
3. Make a one-page story contract: essence, character interest or agenda, possible competing forces, tonal design and major question.
4. Select structure because it helps, not as a default obligation.
5. Offer minimal Markdown project setup from `revision-and-canon.md` if file creation is wanted.

## W2. Import an existing manuscript

Read the available material, inventory file/chapter order, extract confirmed vs inferred vs disputed story facts, record temporal states, identify voice samples and open questions. Preserve all originals. Never infer a whole book from one chapter. Propose a project bible or create it with authorization.

## W3. Build or refine a character

Use the five-layer cognitive model in `character-and-conflict.md`; gather bodily and social detail, independent desire, contradictions, and relationship variations. If extracting from text, attach scene evidence and confidence. If inventing, explicitly mark proposals. Track progressions by time.

## W4. Plot and arc plan

Describe reader promise/essential idea; map desire → pressure → choices → consequences; choose structure or thematic organizing pattern; assign principal and subplot arcs, foreshadowing, payoffs, and rest periods. Mark uncertain chapter placements as hypotheses.

## W5. Write a scene/chapter

Read project canon and relevant adjacent passages if available. Identify POV/time, character objectives, scene purpose, emotional/comic intensity, physical setting, and target length. Draft **prose first** in the expected narrative voice. Audit state-at-time and POV. Avoid tutorials and process narration unless asked. Offer an optional concise change-impact note for inserted scenes.

## W6. Strengthen dialogue

Review the entire relevant exchange. Identify D1–D6 diagnoses if helpful; note speakers' goals and subtext. Test rhythm aloud conceptually, clarify attribution and action beats, suggest revisions that preserve character truth. Do not force indirectness when direct speech is dramatically earned.

## W7. Build comedy into a novel

Identify comic contract and source of absurdity; derive a continuing predicament from relationships; set 2–5 causally escalating social complications if appropriate; use ironic reversals, action and interiority; protect sincere emotional moments; add a transformed callback, if natural; check cumulative consequences. Refer to `comedy-engine.md`. Never impose TV script metrics.

## W8. Critique a passage or chapter

Identify desired effect, read available context, list up to five ranked observations by seriousness, quote or locate evidence briefly, propose fixes without rewriting unless invited, separate facts from uncertainty, and prioritize one or two best interventions.

## W9. Revise an existing passage

Agree level or infer from request; preserve voice and facts; check continuity before broad restructuring, then prose. Deliver revision with change log and unresolved issues. When ambiguity is material, offer alternatives.

## W10. Propagate a canon change

Record exact new fact and effective time; inspect pitch/plot/scenes/entities/manuscript references, output blocking/high/deferrable list, update only owned accessible documents, and preserve change history. If unavailable, produce patches rather than false claims of edits.

## W11. Coach writing

Explain one technique, demonstrate with short original examples, optionally set a concrete writing exercise, and critique against the user's stated effect. Prefer one teachable improvement to a complete grading sheet.

## W12. Evaluate book-level progress

Inventory completed pages/chapters and pending arcs, unresolved reader promises, timeline gaps and character changes. Distinguish writing productivity from prose quality. Propose the smallest next high-value action, respecting the author's preferred balance of planning and discovery.

## W13. Choose a development method for a new novel or light novel

Present the three source-supported development routes (structure-first, character/fragment-first, hybrid), the theme timing options, and the protagonist goal/arc options. Do not overwhelm an author who only requests a short scene. When the writer chooses, record it under `development_method`, `theme_policy`, and `arc_policy` in the project bible. Use `author-decisions.md`.

## W14. Japanese-source techniques in a prose chapter

Select one or two justified techniques from `japanese-methods.md`: fragment collection, diary point of view, small daily deviation, causal leap, reader-as-tsukkomi, apparent non-event, retrospective motif, or relationship-only conflict. Translate into prose with spatial clarity and interior voice. Mention provenance in an explanation or lesson, but not in the story itself.

## W15. Plan a light novel volume

Read confirmed series canon, unresolved chapter questions and current relationship state. Identify this volume's selected promise (comedy/relationship/mystery/adventure/quiet progression) and any inherited reveals. Plan chapters as a rhythm, not a rigid episode equation; distinguish volume payoff from series seed. Draft with `light-novel-craft.md`; verify each chapter against effective-at-time canon.
