# Romantic and ecchi comedy for prose fiction

**Mode:** optional specialized craft module. Load only for author-established romantic comedy, flirtation, suggestive farce or ecchi tone, or an explicit request for this type of scene. This module is not a universal writing philosophy, not a genre switch triggered by character appearance or nationality, and not a requirement that every chapter contain flirtation. It can be used for ordinary novels and light novels alike.

**Provenance:** adapted from `sources/specialized-romantic-comedy/Ecchi Engine Reference v1.md`, `Monster Musume Report.md`, and the partially retrievable `ecchi_quirks_comedy_dynamics.md` (see `references/romantic-comedy-source-ledger.md`). Source works and their scenario examples are analytical references, not templates to copy. Their factual claims about existing series are **not independently verified**. Do not invent missing source material.

## Narrative architecture before titillation

Treat romantic or ecchi comedy as a *story-generating relationship machine*: a character wants a plausible thing; a trait, rule, role or practical task turns the pursuit intimate or awkward; another person has a different interpretation or agenda; attempts to recover dignity or solve the task change the stakes; the episode produces a consequence or an emotionally truthful glimpse. Do not use a mandatory universal beat sheet. See also `references/comedy-engine.md`.

### Three source-model archetypes, not prescriptions

1. **Embodied/domestic complication** (`Monster Musume` analysis): nonhuman physiology or cultural practice complicates an ordinary household task. Adapt: *embodied rule → practical need → social interpretation → changed relationship or consequence*. The fantasy rule should also matter outside flirtation, such as work, access, safety, identity or belonging.
2. **Desire embedded in a power system** (`High School DxD` comparison): characters' preferences, ambitions or emotions interact with magical, institutional or game-like rules. Adapt: *character desire changes what the system costs, enables, or reveals*. Do not import other series' named powers or gender assumptions.
3. **Ritual and obligation** (`Chained Soldier` comparison): a favor, reward, debt, contract or shared ritual becomes a recurring relationship framework. Adapt: *established obligation → personalized negotiation → choice and consequences*. Transactions never substitute for mutual permission; avoid coercion presented as harmless intimacy.

**Original-novel safeguard:** invent distinct rules, cast relationships, humor and narrative stakes. Never lift recognizable scenes, abilities, catchphrases or episode sequences.

## Six complementary comedy channels

- **Situational:** a real practical problem acquires awkward implications.
- **Character:** a characteristic solution predictably makes trouble for that particular person.
- **Reaction:** inner narration, restraint, expressive movement or loss of composure reveals what is concealed.
- **Dialogue:** people speak from different assumptions; subtext and selective honesty do the work.
- **Physical:** position, balance, scale, clothing, props or distinctive capabilities cause grounded consequences.
- **Status:** space, hierarchy, reputation, house rules and relationship roles become contested.

Choose varied channels according to scene stakes; do not add all six to every scene. Build one comic situation in stages rather than firing disconnected punchlines.

## Distinctive character engines

For recurring cast members, define in a project-local profile:

- **Private want** (what they seek from the other person).
- **Public position** (what they admit, deny or perform).
- **Involuntary tell or quirk** (voice, habit, physiology, posture, perception, social role).
- **Countermeasure** (their plan to preserve dignity, consent, competence or status).
- **Self-defeating move** (how the countermeasure worsens exposure or misunderstanding).
- **Relationship-specific variation** (how this works differently with different cast members).
- **Limit and development** (when to retire, reverse or deepen the joke).

An involuntary tell may **suggest** an emotion; it is neither mind reading nor proof of desire or consent. Avoid reducing female characters or any ensemble member to a single sexual trope. Quirks should serve nonromantic characterization too.

## Mutual awareness and failed composure

An especially useful model in `ecchi_quirks_comedy_dynamics.md` is the mismatch between **what a person says**, **what their behavior may imply**, **what the other character thinks they know**, and **what the reader can infer**. Let both parties retain distinctive interpretations. A repetition or mental mantra can become a temporary comic anchor: *focus on the repairs, not the awkward implication*. Its increasing uselessness creates escalation. A gesture intended to conceal attraction can make it more visible. Use sparingly; otherwise it becomes a rigid repetitive voice tic.

Prose-specific tools include limited-perspective perception, free-indirect narration, unreliable self-explanation, physical blocking, timing of paragraph breaks and the contrast between spoken restraint and private thoughts. **Do not automatically force an inner monologue into every suggestive scene.** Omniscient, first-person, close-third, or exterior narration require different handling.

## Escalation without flattening emotion

A reusable *optional* scene route:

1. **Ordinary objective:** the character needs to finish a believable task.
2. **Complicating characteristic:** body, culture, rule, reputation or conflicting desire makes the task socially charged.
3. **Interpretive gap:** characters disagree on what the situation means, or knowingly maintain a convenient fiction.
4. **Countermeasure:** someone tries to fix the visible problem while evading the hidden one.
5. **Backfire:** the fix raises stakes, reveals a contradiction or transfers embarrassment to another character.
6. **Choice:** a person protects someone, admits something, sets a boundary, or decides what matters.
7. **Aftermath:** a small altered expectation, relationship fact, callback seed, or practical rule.

Not every scene needs all seven steps, maximum escalation, interruption or romantic denial. Permit a sincere moment to land without immediately puncturing it. Allow committed relationships and changing ensemble dynamics instead of mandatory endless near-misses.

## From screen/anime grammar to prose

Substitute reader perception for camera concealment, physical detail for visual shorthand, interior commentary for reaction cutaways, and sentence/paragraph rhythm for editing cuts. Do not literalize gags such as cartoon nosebleeds as factual physiology. Do not require object-placed censorship, animation effects, screenplay sluglines or television act lengths. Body-based humor should remain spatially intelligible and physically consistent with the character and setting.

## Boundaries, agency and tone

- Write romance involving adult characters. Attraction, nudity, flirtation and physical closeness are not automatic consent for a further act.
- Make objections, choices, negotiations and changed minds matter. Physiological responses, transformed states and embarrassed reactions do not nullify agency.
- Distinguish intentional flirtation from threats, harassment, coercion or impaired consent. When a source show frames the latter comedically, analyze the distinction rather than reproducing the framing uncritically.
- Do not treat injury, distress or property damage as harmless if the established tone or canon makes it consequential.
- Honor the project's level of sexual explicitness, romantic structure and content boundaries. No universal prohibition on consummation, no universal requirement for it, and no single gendered protagonist archetype.

## Scene and revision checklist

1. Does the desire/awkwardness arise from **these characters and this setting** rather than generic fan service?
2. Does the practical objective remain intelligible?
3. Are power, spatial positions, physical rules, knowledge and relationship statuses consistent?
4. Is the tension carried by premise and choices, not only by repeated accidental contact?
5. Are intimacy and consent distinguishable, and are all characters agents?
6. Does the comic turn vary or change meaning relative to earlier chapters?
7. Is a tender/emotional beat given room if needed?
8. Does the episode move the story, relationships, or reader understanding in an intentional way?
9. Is the prose original rather than a near-copy of any cited series or sample?
