# Principle atlas: source-derived teaching to action

**Read this as a menu, not a recipe.** Each P-number is a usable interpretation of a supplied principle. `S##:L##` points to `moore-source-ledger.md`. **[T]** denotes a Moore-attributed transcript; **[Q]** a Moore quote within the secondary article; **[C]** a journalist's commentary; **[A]** the skill author's application. Examples and evaluation criteria are [A], even when the principle is [T] or [Q].

## Meaning, narrative and immersion

### P01. Separate the essential idea from the plot

- **Source:** S02:L23-L35 [Q] for Moore's distinction at L25; surrounding elaboration [C].
- **Principle:** The central thing a story is *about* differs from the sequence of events.
- **Use:** State the central human or conceptual tension in a sentence; separately list causal events that test it. For a chapter, ask how its choices complicate that tension.
- **Avoid:** Forcing an explicit slogan into dialogue or asking for a thesis before polishing a local paragraph.
- **Test:** If the plot summary changes, can the author still articulate a stable idea? If the idea is clear, are events doing more than illustrating it mechanically?

### P08. Enter an already functioning world

- **Source:** S03:L11 [T], with S03:L7 [T].
- **Principle:** A reader may enjoy reconstructing an unfamiliar social reality from context without a preface explaining it all.
- **Use:** Open amid an intelligible action with selective unexplained terms or institutions; clarify through stakes and later context.
- **Avoid:** Confusion that conceals basic agency, geography, or the immediate conflict. Not every novel must open in medias res.
- **Test:** Can a reader identify what someone wants, even if they cannot yet name every custom or object?

### P09. Let background emerge through the world

- **Source:** S02:L45-L49 [Q] for Moore's caution about expository captions/dialogue; the journalist's extension is [C].
- **Principle:** Direct explanation is often less immersive than showing facts as used or encountered.
- **Use:** Reveal systems through work, purchases, mistakes, customs, public notices, or choices with consequences.
- **Avoid:** Withholding necessary orientation or treating all exposition as a defect. Purposeful explanation can be efficient.
- **Test:** Would people in this world naturally say or think these details now?

### P21. Keep transitions from breaking the fictive experience

- **Source:** S02:L37-L43 [Q] for the quoted observation; further discussion [C].
- **Principle:** An awkward transition can make readers suddenly aware of the machinery of storytelling.
- **Use:** Make time, location, viewpoint, emotional carryover, and causal connection legible where needed; vary between bridges and purposeful hard cuts.
- **Avoid:** Connecting every scene with repetitive travel or explanatory filler. Intentional disorientation is valid when controlled.
- **Test:** Can a reader follow the change without stopping to reconstruct accidental ambiguities?

## Character and empathy

### P02. Inhabit a character's body and mind

- **Source:** S04:L2-L4 [T]; Moore enactment quoted in S02:L63-L71 [Q], commentary [C].
- **Principle:** Imagination and research should make a perspective experientially specific, including how a body limits and shapes action.
- **Use:** Ask how the character moves, senses, sits, speaks, tires, misreads, desires, and copes with physical surroundings. Write consequences, not diagnostic labels or trait lists.
- **Avoid:** Stereotyped assumptions about real disabilities, mental states, cultures, or bodies. Research and sensitivity matter.
- **Test:** Could this passage plausibly belong to any character, or does the experience arise from *this* person's body/history?

### P03. Give antagonists intelligible motives without excusing harm

- **Source:** S04:L6 [T]; S06:L7 [T].
- **Principle:** Flat villains diminish the resonance of protagonists; empathy helps reveal how antagonists rationalize their actions.
- **Use:** Identify competing goods, fears, loyalties, appetites, and self-justifications. Show consequences honestly.
- **Avoid:** Symmetry that erases ethical differences, whitewashes abuse, or forces an antagonist into redemption.
- **Test:** Can a reader explain why the antagonist believes their conduct makes sense, while still judging it?

### P04. Let every significant character be a protagonist to themselves

- **Source:** S04:L6-L8 [T].
- **Principle:** Characters carry independent agendas and should retain integrity even when the plot pressures them.
- **Use:** Before dialogue, name what each participant thinks the scene is for. Let their differing stories collide. Revisit an outline if a choice would violate established motivation.
- **Avoid:** Giving every minor passerby equal page time, or surrendering narrative design to arbitrary detours.
- **Test:** Would the character act this way if the nominal protagonist were not present?

## Place, research and worldmaking

### P05. Place participates in the formation of character

- **Source:** S03:L1, L5 [T].
- **Principle:** Environment is not scenery alone; local history, built space, and collective meaning affect psychology.
- **Use:** Show how the place organizes memory, status, aspiration, danger, and habit. Ask what someone raised here assumes is normal.
- **Avoid:** Imposing a single deterministic personality on everyone from the same place.
- **Test:** If the story were moved elsewhere, what important motives or actions would change?

### P06. Earn specificity through pleasurable research

- **Source:** S03:L3, L9 [T]; S04:L2 [T]; S06:L3 [T].
- **Principle:** Concrete knowledge of geography, history, practices, and institutions produces convincing details.
- **Use:** Research only the high-leverage unknowns for the task; track confirmed facts versus invented ones. Choose telling details rather than displaying a research dump.
- **Avoid:** Confidently inventing real period details, legal rules, procedures, medicine, dialects, or locations. Do not stall all creativity for endless research.
- **Test:** Are vivid details compatible with the chosen place and period, and do they actually influence the scene?

### P07. Sustain fictional-world logic and everyday texture

- **Source:** S03:L7 [T].
- **Principle:** An invented world can be wildly different from ours if internally consistent; its mundane cultural residue makes it inhabited.
- **Use:** Maintain explicit rules and consequences. Seed artifacts such as notices, songs, slogans, procedures, jokes, and rituals only when appropriate.
- **Avoid:** Contradictory magic/technology/social rules and gratuitous invented vocabulary.
- **Test:** Can the reader infer stable rules from repeated consequences? Do details suggest life beyond the current plot?

## Genre, mode, narrator

### P10. Know a genre well enough to work against it

- **Source:** S05:L2-L4 [T].
- **Principle:** Familiarity reveals genre conventions that can be bent, resisted, or blended.
- **Use:** Identify promises readers expect and decide which to fulfill, distort, or refuse. Mixed-genre stories may contain shifts between comedy, romance, tragedy, suspense, and horror.
- **Avoid:** Subversion as surprise for its own sake; abandoning the intended readership's basic contract without reason.
- **Test:** What is the author making possible by keeping or breaking this convention?

### P11. Choose a mode and manipulate expectation intentionally

- **Source:** S05:L6 [T].
- **Principle:** Comedy and horror can both work through expectation and reversal, differentiated by the affect produced.
- **Use:** Before a beat, predict what the reader expects. Control framing, timing, stakes, and aftermath to produce laughter, dread, tenderness, or mixtures.
- **Avoid:** Reducing comedy or horror to a formula, or inserting a punch line into an intentionally sincere moment.
- **Test:** Is the reversal prepared, understandable, and emotionally aligned with the scene?

### P12. Choose tense, person, and narrative distance for effect

- **Source:** S05:L8-L10 [T].
- **Principle:** Past/present tense, first/third person, and close/remote distance produce different forms of access and immediacy.
- **Use:** Maintain the authorized POV contract; in close third, filter perception and diction through the focal character; in omniscient, establish an intentional narrator. Third person does not automatically grant omniscience.
- **Avoid:** Inadvertent head-hopping and tense drift. Neither first person nor present tense is inherently superior.
- **Test:** Does every reported thought/perception have a legitimate source under the chosen POV?

### P13. Let subjectivity, unreliability, and free indirect discourse serve character

- **Source:** S05:L8-L10 [T].
- **Principle:** Narrative access can expose the gap between the character's telling and reality; close third can carry a character's phrasing without explicit thought tags.
- **Use:** Shape habitual omissions, recurring internal arguments, self-protective reinterpretations, and subtle linguistic filters.
- **Avoid:** Unmotivated concealment by the narrator or ambiguous viewpoint switches that look like errors.
- **Test:** Can readers sense the character's biased consciousness without mistaking accidental author confusion for artistry?

## Language and attention

### P14. Seek fresh, surprising verbal combinations

- **Source:** S01:L2, L6-L8 [T], overlapping S07:L6-L8 [T].
- **Principle:** Unfamiliar juxtaposition and flexible word use can arrest a reader's attention and resist cliché.
- **Use:** Replace stale phraseology with exact, character-appropriate imagery. Make surprise meaningful to perception, mood, or theme.
- **Avoid:** Constant ornament, lexical noise, anachronistic metaphors, or language the character could not plausibly think.
- **Test:** Does an unexpected phrase deepen the scene, or merely demand attention for itself?

### P15. Arrange words and sensory cues to direct imagined experience

- **Source:** S07:L2-L4 [T]. Its specific neuroscience assertions are *not independently evidenced*.
- **Principle:** Reader images change incrementally as words arrive; concrete physical description can produce embodied responses.
- **Use:** Sequence perceptual information so each detail meaningfully revises a mental image. Write pressure, weight, movement, orientation, and apprehension concretely.
- **Avoid:** Claiming that a given construction triggers proven neural responses, or describing every movement in slow motion.
- **Test:** Does the sentence create the intended image in the intended order without forcing re-reading?

### P16. Use contradiction deliberately to produce estrangement

- **Source:** S01:L4 [T], overlapping S07:L12 [T].
- **Principle:** Incompatible descriptive cues can deny the reader a coherent picture, sometimes creating the uncanny.
- **Use:** In horror, dream logic, or altered consciousness, vary sensory cues to destabilize perception intentionally.
- **Avoid:** Treating genuine continuity or anatomy mistakes as avant-garde technique; don't use incoherence in scenes requiring clarity.
- **Test:** Is the reader's uncertainty about the *phenomenon*, not about which basic object is being described?

### P17. Use formal constraints to discover possibilities

- **Source:** S01:L10 [T], overlapping S07:L14 [T].
- **Principle:** Deliberate restrictions can force new verbal and conceptual decisions.
- **Use:** Try a short exercise with one missing sense, a forbidden cliché, no speech tags, a narrow focal vocabulary, or a repeated object, then assess gains and losses.
- **Avoid:** Forcing whole novels to follow a constraint the author has not chosen or maintaining an exercise after it stops helping.
- **Test:** Did the constraint uncover a stronger image, rhythm, or narrative possibility?

## Judgment, ethics, persistence

### P18. Balance earth, sword, cup, and wand

- **Source:** S06:L1-L9 [T].
- **Principle:** Material understanding and conditions, discriminating intellect, compassion, and will are complementary writerly resources. Their tarot imagery is metaphorical here.
- **Use:** Before major work, check research reality, critical choice, empathic modeling, and what will get completed.
- **Avoid:** Treating the symbols as supernatural prescriptions or judging a writer's worth by productivity.
- **Test:** Is a stalled task lacking reliable context, a firm decision, human insight, or commitment to a finite deliverable?

### P19. Commit work to completion and external appraisal

- **Source:** S06:L9-L13 [T].
- **Principle:** Will, accountability, and actually finishing matter; an endlessly imagined novel remains unfinished.
- **Use:** Choose a finite next artifact, define done, and defer tempting additions to a parking lot until the draft is complete.
- **Avoid:** Pressuring exhausted authors or treating arbitrary speed as quality.
- **Test:** Can the author finish and evaluate the selected unit without inventing three new prerequisites?

### P20. Attempt creative work beyond habitual certainty

- **Source:** S02:L73-L77 [Q] at L75, plus commentary [C].
- **Principle:** Challenge and the possibility of interesting failure can be more fertile than competent repetition.
- **Use:** Offer one bounded experiment or unexpected alternative when the author invites exploration.
- **Avoid:** Treating risk as an obligation or discarding effective conventions simply because they are familiar.
- **Test:** Does the experiment enlarge the author's options and teach something recoverable?

### P22. Keep human sympathy ahead of reputation or cleverness

- **Source:** S02:L79-L87 [Q] for the quoted remarks; S04:L6-L8 [T] and S06:L7 [T] support empathetic characterization.
- **Principle:** The article quotes Moore warning against the trap of reputation and emphasizing human goodness over writerly status.
- **Use:** Prioritize the author's authentic aims, ethical care in representation, and full human complexity rather than aesthetic performance.
- **Avoid:** Converting a broad ethical aspiration into a compulsory sermon or censorship of difficult fiction.
- **Test:** Does the work grant people dimensionality without using them merely as props or prestige signals?

## Adaptation register: procedures that Moore did not prescribe

These are **[A]**, even though informed by the atlas:

| Adapter | Use | Informed by |
|---|---|---|
| A01: Scene pressure map | Track each participant's want, perceived stakes, obstacles, choice, and consequence. | P01, P03, P04, P11 |
| A02: Chapter contribution check | Before inserting a chapter, identify what uniquely changes in plot, character, place, or reader knowledge. | P01, P08, P19, P21 |
| A03: Canon and continuity ledger | Preserve fixed facts, timeline, narrative contract, and established implications across long projects. | P04, P07, P12 |
| A04: Revision triage | Fix contradictions and major motive/structure problems before polishing syntax; use targeted passes. | P03, P07, P14, P21 |
| A05: Wordplay dosage | Reserve heightened language for moments where increased strangeness serves character and effect. | P14-P17 |
| A06: Coaching loop | Explain, demonstrate, assign exercise, observe, give targeted feedback, repeat or escalate. | P02, P17, P18 |
| A07: Diagnostic rubric | Evaluate reader effects with qualitative anchors and optional 1-4 ratings; never equate scores with objective artistic merit. | P01-P22 |

No adapter should be attributed as Moore's own phrasing, formula, or guaranteed method.
