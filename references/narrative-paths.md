# Select an approach before imposing a structure

Read `author-decisions.md` to distinguish unresolved author preferences from methodological alternatives.

## Structure-first route

Choose when the author wants an outline, mystery engineering, complex foreshadowing, tight adventure pacing or predictable draft scope. Define intended reader experience, core promise or thematic hypothesis, protagonist's main line of action, opposing forces, endpoint or transformation, and major reveals. Pick a lens such as three-act, four-movement, Truby's organic steps or a bespoke volume arc. Draft backwards from consequences to necessary setup and then forward for lived continuity. Do not assume a screenplay page count translates to novel word count.

**Origin:** `sw-story-structure`, `sw-truby-anatomy`, `sw-premise-theme`, plus the structure-first group in `sw-japanese-screenwriting`.

## Character/fragment-first route

Choose when the author cares more about authentic voice, exploratory character chemistry, slice-of-life events, comedy born of personalities or discovering a story's emotional center through writing. Begin with a compelling person, relationship, place, sensory memory, conversational rhythm, contradictory everyday detail or odd object. Generate scenes as **evidence**. After several scenes, identify emerging repetitions, pressures, questions and possible ordering. Outline only to the degree the author wants. A scene can be valuable before its structural function is known.

**Origin:** Miki, Yokohama, Takahashi, Sono, Fukuda and Omiya as described in `sw-japanese-screenwriting`; Moore's character agency and place attention.

## Hybrid route

Choose a provisional reader promise, possible endpoint and a small cast/setting map. Maintain an open beat list rather than a locked outline. Draft chapters while recording new canon and emerging theme; update the outline as discoveries warrant. Use both foreshadowing discipline and freedom for genuine character surprise. Label changes to canon or author-approved plan.

## Preserving reader promises across routes

Whatever route is chosen, track what the prose **asks the reader to expect**: mystery, romance development, comedic social friction, revenge, psychological insight, atmospheric discovery, or a set of episodic pleasures. A scene that postpones a promise may still be worthwhile, but serial delay without development can strain trust. Author-approved intentional ambiguity is not a factual error.

## Japanese and screenwriting terms

`起承転結` (kishōtenketsu) may be useful to examine introduction, development, turn and conclusion; the supplied Japanese skill writes it with Chinese shorthand `起承转合` and discusses its relation to three acts. Do not impose strict identity among these systems. **Use them as ways to notice form**, not universal templates. The same principle applies to Snyder beats, McKee value turns, and Truby's 22 steps.

## Choosing within an ongoing project

Do not interrogate the author each chapter. Read `story.md` for `development_method`, `theme_policy`, and `arc_policy` if they exist. If absent and the task is limited, respond using the established prose. If building a new project and the route materially changes the deliverable, offer clear alternatives for the author to select.
