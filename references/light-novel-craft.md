# Light novel mode: original prose adaptation

**Status:** This module is a **new synthesis** for the user's goal, informed by but not literally taught by the archived Japanese screenwriting skills. No source in the bundle is a complete, verified how-to manual for Japanese light novel prose. Treat every convention as a selectable reader promise, not a cultural rule.

## Establish the volume's voice contract

Choose and record: target age/readership if known; first/close-third/omniscient POV; past/present tense; narrator self-awareness; length and density of descriptive paragraphs; speed of plot advancement; inner monologue; level of comic exaggeration; degrees of romance, mystery, action and quiet life; illustration-inspired staging where relevant; chapter and volume rhythm. The author can write literary, dense, gothic, spare, comedic or cinematic light-novel prose.

## Novel prose rather than screenplay

- A visual gag requires **perceivable prose**: specific line of sight, position, timing, body language, or narrator reaction. Don't substitute bare `CUT TO`, `INT.`, SFX labels or cinematic shot lists for sentences unless the author deliberately chooses typographic experimentation.
- Build a character's thought process alongside their speech. Internal reaction may be a comic counterpunch, self-deception, tenderness or tension, but it must remain true to POV.
- Give striking character introductions physical specificity **as the POV would perceive it**. Distinctive appearance should be usable, not generic adjective stacking. Do not dump every known fact into a first encounter; give enough for the intended vivid impression and spread details across interaction.
- In action, spatial causality outranks speed. Map who is where, what is held, what changes, who has knowledge, and how consequences follow.
- Dialogue-heavy passages benefit from physical beats, altered intent, quiet interruptions, subtext and occasional narration; not every line needs a joke or a quip.

## Chapters, volumes and series

Chapters may form episodes with a satisfying local turn, a promise carried forward, or a quiet thematic completion. The volume should carry a meaningful emotional or relational movement even if the plot is episodic. Across volumes maintain character-state-at-time, outstanding questions, foreshadowing, world mechanics, relationship progression, and a clear record of what each viewpoint character knows. Repetition of a familiar situation is acceptable only when the variation, reader pleasure or emotional implication is purposeful.

## Practical narrative timing

- Use a chapter opening that establishes **interest**, which may be a comic interruption, a question, a consequential choice, a striking sensory mismatch, or a familiar ritual with one unsettling change.
- After dense information, give breathing room; after intense action, permit recovery. In comedy, delayed comprehension and an apparently irrelevant detail can create a punchline.
- Use dramatic irony and reader inference; don't have characters explain the joke after it lands.
- For long conversations, track the person currently attempting to change the other's behavior. Let subject changes reveal relationship dynamics rather than transcribing unstructured small talk.
- Use cliffhangers only where they deepen a genuine promise; not every chapter must end with an artificial shock.

## Genre and tone options

- **Comedic fantasy:** character flaws + ordinary logistics inside an extraordinary world + escalating cross-purposes + earned emotional silence.
- **Romance:** reciprocal desire and agency + boundaries + changing interpretations of ordinary gestures + delayed/partial disclosure.
- **Adventure:** actionable immediate objective + coherent space + reversals that force adaptation + relationship consequence.
- **Slice of life:** pattern, variance, sensory world and interior concern; resist imported mandatory peril or moral conversion.
- **Drama/horror:** embodied uncertainty, selective disclosure, contrast between ordinary routine and changed stakes; avoid joke dilution.

## Three checks before delivery

1. **Read as prose:** Would the paragraph work without pictures, voice acting or a storyboard?
2. **Read as this narrator:** Are diction, imagery, humor and perception consistent with the chosen character?
3. **Read as this volume:** Does it preserve facts already established, advance or deepen the intended experience, and make no unearned promise?

The author may ask for more cinematic, atmospheric, humorous, minimalist or experimental writing; respond to their explicit aesthetic over these suggestions.
